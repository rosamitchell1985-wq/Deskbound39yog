Health disclaimer

Last updated: August 24, 2026.

Stop and see a doctor instead of stretching if you have any of these

  • Numbness or tingling running down a leg — especially if it goes past the knee, or if it follows a line down the back or side of the leg into the foot.
  • Weakness in a leg or foot — a foot that drags or slaps, a knee that gives way, trouble pushing up onto your toes or lifting the front of your foot.
  • Pain that started with an injury — a fall, a car accident, a lift that went wrong, or any impact.
  • Pain that wakes you at night, or that is clearly worse lying down and does not ease with a change of position.

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room if back pain comes with loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or inner thighs, sudden severe weakness in both legs, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Also get evaluated for: unexplained weight loss, fever or chills with back pain, a history of cancer, or pain that has been getting steadily worse for more than a few weeks. The AAOS lists several of these as warning signs that call for prompt medical attention rather than self-care.

That is the claim, and it stops there.

Talk to a provider before you start if

  • You are being treated for a back, neck, hip or shoulder problem, or you have had surgery on any of them.
  • You have a diagnosed disc problem, spinal stenosis, spondylolisthesis, osteoporosis or inflammatory arthritis.
  • You are pregnant or recently gave birth.
  • You have a heart condition, uncontrolled high blood pressure, or you get dizzy on standing.
  • You have balance problems, a vestibular disorder, or a history of falls.
  • You are recovering from any recent injury or illness.

A physical therapist is usually the right person for a persistent desk-related complaint, and in most states you can see one without a referral. That is generally a better use of your time than another month of guessing on the internet.

Stop rules while you are moving

  • Sharp is a stop sign. Dull, stretchy, mildly uncomfortable is normal. Sharp, electric, burning or shooting is not.
  • Anything travelling down a limb — stop that move and do not repeat it.
  • Dizziness, light-headedness, chest pain or shortness of breath — stop, sit down, and seek care if it does not clear quickly.
  • Pain that is worse the next day and keeps getting worse each time you do a move — drop that move. Movement that consistently makes something worse is information, not a challenge to push through.
