About Deskbound

A small publisher in Boise, Idaho. One writer, one timer, four routines, and a strong preference for saying only what we can actually stand behind.

Why this exists

I spent fourteen years as a database engineer. By the end of it I had the standard package: a low back that objected to standing up after a long meeting, a band of ache across the top of my shoulders every Thursday, and a hip that clicked on the walk from the parking garage. I bought a good chair. It helped a little. I bought a standing desk and stood for three weeks straight, at which point my feet took over the complaining.

What eventually worked was unglamorous and cheap: getting out of the chair more often, and spending a few minutes doing the opposite of whatever I had just been doing for two hours. I trained as a yoga teacher afterward, mostly because that is where the largest library of no-equipment positions lives, and I have spent the years since teaching movement to people who work at desks.

Deskbound is the version of that I would have wanted at 34: a page that opens fast on a phone, tells you exactly what to do for five minutes, and does not ask you to buy, subscribe, journal or believe anything.

What we will not do

This matters more than the mission statement, so here it is plainly.

  • No medical claims. Nothing here treats, cures or prevents anything. It is movement. Movement can change how an area feels and moves. That is what we will say and nothing more.
  • No invented credentials. I am not a physical therapist, a physician or a researcher, and nothing on this site should be read as coming from one. Where a factual claim needs backing, it gets a link to a public source you can check — OSHA, MedlinePlus, AAOS OrthoInfo, CDC.
  • No made-up numbers. You will not find a statistic on this site that we cannot point at. If a number is a rule of thumb rather than a finding, we say so — the 20-8-2 rule on the desk setup page is labeled exactly that.
  • No streaks, badges or guilt. There is no account, no progress tracking and no email that asks where you have been. Missing three days is not a relapse.
  • No wellness voice. No journeys, no Sanskrit, no namaste. It's mobility work that happens to come from yoga, and it should read like the instructions for a tool.

How the site is built

Deliberately plain. Static HTML, one stylesheet, one script, no framework and no tracking pixels. The five-minute timer runs entirely in your browser: it does not call a server, so it works on an airplane, in a basement office with two bars, or with the network off. Nothing you type into a form on this site is transmitted anywhere in the current build.

The timer computes elapsed time from the clock rather than by counting ticks, which sounds like a footnote and is the reason it stays correct when you lock your phone in the middle of move three and come back forty seconds later. That was the first thing we fixed, because it was the first thing everyone hit.

How it's funded

Advertising, clearly labeled, in three fixed positions on the content pages. That is the whole business model. There is no course, no membership tier, no affiliate storefront, and no sponsored routine. If a product is ever mentioned in a way that earns money, that will be stated in the sentence where it is mentioned, not in a footer.

Ads are kept away from the timer controls and the navigation on purpose. Nobody should ever tap an ad while reaching for Pause.

Where the photos come from

Real photographs, licensed for commercial use from Pexels, showing people at actual desks in actual clothes. No studio wellness imagery, no stock model on a beach at sunrise. If a photo shows someone doing a move, they are doing it in a way you could copy at your own desk.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, tell us and we will fix it and say that we did. Every content page carries a “last reviewed” date so you can see when it was last read end to end rather than just published. Reach us at hello@deskbound.co or through the contact page.
