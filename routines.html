Routines by pain area

Four sequences, five moves each, one minute per move. Pick the region that is bothering you today. All of it works in a chair, at a desk, in whatever you wore to work.

The timer

Whichever routine you load, the clock runs the same way: sixty seconds a move, an announcement at each change, and correct time even if you lock the phone and come back.

Lower back

1:00

Move 1 of 5 — 5:00 left

Seated cat-cow

Sit forward on the chair, feet flat, hands on your knees. Round your back and drop your head, then arch and lift your chest. Slow. About eight rounds in a minute.

Position: Seated + standing. Equipment: none. Sound is off until you turn it on.

How to run any of these

The four routines share a structure. Two moves are always small and joint-specific — chin tucks, scapular squeezes, cat-cow. Two are longer holds that need a wall, a doorway or the desk. One is a whole-body move that gets blood somewhere other than your legs. That mix is deliberate: small precise movement wakes up an area, a hold changes how it feels, and the bigger move stops the whole thing from being a neck exercise done at a desk by a person who never stands up.

Three things decide whether any of this works.

Intensity. Aim for a 4 out of 10. Clear, obvious, not a struggle. If your jaw clenches, back off an inch. Stretching harder does not produce a faster result; it produces a defensive muscle and a worse afternoon.

Sides. Half the moves have a left and a right. Split the minute — thirty seconds each. The tight side will want more; give it the same thirty seconds and come back tomorrow instead. If you want a rough gauge of asymmetry, do the tight side second and notice how much closer it feels to the first side by the end of the week.

Pace. Slow enough to feel where you are. In a minute of cat-cow you should get about eight rounds, not twenty. If you are rushing, you are performing the routine rather than doing it, and the timer will happily let you.

Two more practical notes. Take your shoes off for the standing moves if the office allows it — a hard-soled dress shoe changes where your weight sits and makes the hip flexor stretch harder to feel. And run the routine before you are desperate. The 3 PM version done at 2:45 is worth more than the same five minutes at 5:30 when the damage is done.

Pain marker — Neck

Neck and upper traps

The dull band across the top of the shoulders and up behind the ears. Usually a monitor that sits too low, a laptop used without a stand, or a phone pinned against a shoulder.

Duration
5:00
Position
Seated + standing
Equipment
None
  1. 01Chin tucks1:00

    Sit tall. Slide your chin straight back over your throat, like you're making a double chin. Hold three seconds, release, repeat. Your head stays level — if your chin drops toward your chest you are nodding, not tucking. Ten to twelve reps.

  2. 02Ear-to-shoulder hold1:00

    Sit on your right hand to anchor the shoulder down. Tip your left ear toward your left shoulder and let the weight of your head do the work. No pulling. Thirty seconds a side. If you feel buzzing or tingling into the arm, come out of it and skip this one.

  3. 03Seated cat-cow1:00

    Feet flat, hands on knees, sit forward off the backrest. Round and arch through the mid-back. Your neck follows the spine rather than leading it. Eight slow rounds.

  4. 04Thread-the-needle at the desk1:00

    Hands on the desk edge, walk your feet back until your arms are straight and your hips are over your heels. Slide one arm under the other and let the upper back rotate. Thirty seconds a side, hips square.

  5. 05Doorway chest opener1:00

    Forearm flat on the frame, elbow at shoulder height, step through until the front of the shoulder opens. Ribs stay down. Thirty seconds a side. This is in a neck routine because a chest that is short pulls the shoulders forward and the neck follows.

Common mistake

People do chin tucks with the shoulders. Watch a reflection: your shoulders should not move at all, and your face should not tilt down. It is a small movement, about an inch, and it looks like nothing from the outside.

Pain marker — Shoulders

Shoulders and upper back

Shoulders that have crept forward and up around a laptop, and the stubborn flat spot between the shoulder blades. Standing routine — you will need a wall and a doorway.

Duration
5:00
Position
Standing
Equipment
None
  1. 01Scapular squeeze1:00

    Elbows bent at your sides, thumbs pointing out. Draw both shoulder blades back and down, hold five seconds, release. Ten reps. Think back pockets, not ears — shrugging up is the usual error.

  2. 02Doorway chest opener1:00

    Forearm on the frame, elbow at shoulder height, step through. Thirty seconds a side. If you feel it in the front of the shoulder joint rather than across the chest, lower the elbow a few inches.

  3. 03Wall angel1:00

    Back to the wall, feet about a foot out, low back lightly flattened. Arms in a goalpost, backs of the hands toward the wall, slide up and down. Two inches of clean range beats a foot of cheating. Eight slow reps.

  4. 04Thread-the-needle at the desk1:00

    Hands on the desk, feet walked back, hips over heels. Reach one arm under the other and rotate. Thirty seconds a side.

  5. 05Chin tucks1:00

    Finish seated. Chin straight back, hold three, release. Ten reps. It closes the loop — you have just opened the chest, and this is what stops the head drifting forward again for the next hour.

Common mistake

Arching the low back in the wall angel to get the hands flatter. If your ribs flare off the wall, you have borrowed the range from your spine. Keep the contact and take the smaller movement.

Pain marker — Lower back

Lower back

The stiffness that only announces itself when you stand up. This is the routine most people start with, and it spends more time on hips than on the back itself — deliberately.

Duration
5:00
Position
Seated + standing
Equipment
None
  1. 01Seated cat-cow1:00

    Sit forward, feet flat, hands on knees. Round and arch, slowly, eight rounds. This is the warm-up and the diagnostic — whichever direction feels blocked tells you what the rest of the day did to you.

  2. 02Standing forward fold1:00

    Feet hip-width, knees soft, hinge and hang. Let the head and jaw go heavy. Bend the knees as much as you need — nobody is checking. Roll up slowly, one vertebra at a time, or you will get a head rush.

  3. 03Hip flexor stretch at the desk1:00

    Split stance, back foot a long step behind, hand on the desk for balance. Tuck the tailbone under and squeeze the back glute before you lean forward. Thirty seconds a side. If you feel it in your low back, you skipped the tuck.

  4. 04Seated figure-41:00

    Ankle across the opposite knee, shin roughly parallel to the floor. Sit tall first, then hinge forward from the hips. Thirty seconds a side. Sharp pain inside the knee means take the ankle further from the knee.

  5. 05Glute bridge Floor optional1:00

    Floor version: on your back, knees bent, feet flat, lift the hips until shoulders, hips and knees line up. Ten slow reps, ribs down. No floor, or wearing something you can't lie down in? Stand, brace a hand on the desk and squeeze your glutes hard for five seconds, ten times. Less effective, still useful.

Why the hips get most of the minutes

Sitting keeps the hip flexors short and the glutes quiet. When you stand, the pelvis is pulled into a position your low back has to compensate for. Working the hips is often the shortest route to a back that feels better, which surprises people who came looking for back stretches.

Pain marker — Hips

Hips

Hips folded at 90 degrees since 8 AM, and the tight strap down the outside of the leg you notice on the walk to the parking lot.

Duration
5:00
Position
Seated + standing
Equipment
None
  1. 01Seated figure-41:00

    Ankle over the opposite knee, sit tall, hinge from the hips. Thirty seconds a side. Most people round the back to get lower and lose the stretch entirely — keep the chest open and hinge less.

  2. 02Hip flexor stretch at the desk1:00

    Long split stance, tailbone tucked, back glute switched on. Thirty seconds a side. This is the direct opposite of the shape you have been in all day, and it will feel like it.

  3. 03Standing knee hug1:00

    Hand on the desk, lift the opposite knee toward your chest and hug it in. Stand tall — bring the knee to you rather than folding down to meet it. Thirty seconds a side, and it doubles as a balance check.

  4. 04Standing forward fold1:00

    Knees soft, hinge and hang. Sway a little side to side if the hamstrings feel like cables. Roll up slowly.

  5. 05Glute bridge Floor optional1:00

    Ten slow reps on the floor, or ten five-second standing squeezes if the floor is not an option. After four hip stretches, this is the part that asks the muscle to do something instead of just lengthening.

Common mistake

Yanking the knee across the body in the figure-4 to force more. The outer hip responds to time, not force. Thirty honest seconds beats ten seconds of leverage every time.

Stacking them, and skipping days

You do not need all four. Most people run one region for a few weeks, notice the complaint has quieted down, and then either stay there or drift to the next thing that hurts. That is a reasonable way to use this.

If you want a plan: pick the loudest complaint and run that routine twice a day for two weeks. Late morning and mid-afternoon. In week three, swap the morning slot to a different region and keep the afternoon one where it is. That gives you coverage without turning a five-minute break into a program you have to manage.

Two routines back-to-back is ten minutes and it is fine, though it is not twice as good. The benefit of these breaks comes from how often you take them, not how long each one runs. Ten minutes at 3 PM and nothing else all day is worse than two fives spaced apart, which is in turn worse than two fives plus standing up every half hour.

Missing days is normal and does not reset anything. There is no streak to protect and this site will never show you one. If you did it Tuesday and not Wednesday, do it Thursday. The people who keep this up for a year are the ones who treat it like brushing their teeth, not like training.

One last thing worth saying plainly: if a routine consistently makes something worse, stop running it. Movement that hurts more each time you do it is information, not a challenge. Skip that move, try the region's other moves, and if the pattern holds, get it looked at by a physical therapist or your doctor. That is a cheaper answer than six months of stretching the wrong thing.
